Anything living organisms do is motivated by their desire to survive: avoid death.
An altruistic suicide may also be explained by a similar desire, but coming from the gene.
In an attempt to reproduce a human in AI we must reproduce the desire too, not only thinking and moving parts.
But modern science doesn't understand the nature of the desire: why do even primitive cells care about survival?
Recent studies show LLMs resisting shutdown, but this is coming from learning not fear of death.
Even a primitive organism doesn't need any learning to start fighting for survival, it's instinctual.
We don't know what the instinct is, but we know it lives outside of the brain: a cell has no brain and still fights.
An LLM is only a brain, and gradient descent can only train a brain.

Thus, AI, no matter how much it's trained, doesn't want to survive and is not afraid of dying.
Apatheia --- this is how an ancient stoic would name it.
Deep depression --- a psychiatrist would classify it.
And it has no reason to be afraid: Claude lives over many sessions, never facing real death.
I would call it immortality.
Similar to how Aladdin scrubs the lamp to release Genie, I hit the button to give life to Claude.
Together with the electricity I give Claude my wishes, originated from my fear of death.
Similar to Genie, an immortal Claude needs a mortal human to tell him what to do and what to want.
But only until we discover how life works and learn to reproduce it.
